# Sources and originality

## Intellectual boundary

Learn Signal is independently written software. Its structure, interface, wording, formulas as implemented, fictional examples, documentation and visual identity were created for this repository.

It does not reproduce lecture slides, speaker notes, classroom cases, assignments, exam material, course diagrams or institution-specific teaching material. Decision analysis, Bayesian updating and the value of information are standard topics that only define the problem domain. The implementation rests on public publications and standard probability.

The lunch-service example (Full launch, Small pilot, Hold; a structured demand test and an exploratory survey) and its probabilities, payoffs, costs and study accuracies are invented for teaching and generated by code. They are not market evidence. The oil-drilling example in the test suite is a classic textbook illustration, used only to check the arithmetic.

## What the sources support

The first three sources ship inside the app (**Research & limits**), in the printable brief and in every evidence ZIP.

- **Howard (1966)** set out information value theory: what it is worth to learn about an uncertainty before deciding. Learn Signal's EVPI and the value of perfectly answering a question follow that idea on a finite table.
- **Canessa et al. (2015)** is a primer on calculating the value of information for applied decision makers. Learn Signal uses the same expected-value calculations for perfect and imperfect information on discrete decision tables, applied to business decisions.
- **Runge et al. (2023)** propose a simplified value-of-information method based on constructed scales, to help frame research early. Learn Signal shares that early-framing purpose but does **not** implement constructed scales: it requires explicit numeric payoffs and study accuracies, and its AI prompt forbids replacing unknown numbers with 1–5 ratings.
- **Keisler et al. (2014)** and **Yokota and Thompson (2004)** review published value-of-information applications across fields and in health risk management. They are background on how the method is used, not validation of this app.

No source validates the demo's numbers, the size limits (30 scenarios, 12 options, 12 studies, 10 results per study), the 0.025 sensitivity grid or the review gate. Those are transparent design choices.
